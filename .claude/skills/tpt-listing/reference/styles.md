# Style menu

Each product in a store should look like its own thing while staying recognizable as the same seller.
Before designing, look at the seller's other covers (ask for them if you haven't seen any) and pick a style
that **isn't** one they already use. Match the style to the topic's era or theme.

| Style | Looks like | Fonts (Google) | Palette | Background | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vintage playbill** | Circus or showboat poster, woodtype | Rye, Alfa Slab One, Lobster | cream, barn red, navy, gold | cream with faded sunburst rays, double-line frame | 1700s–1800s, games, anything theatrical |
| **Bright pop burst** | Bold flat promo, sticker cut-out portrait | Lilita One, Bowlby One, Inter | sky blue, sunflower, coral, navy | saturated sunburst, diagonal color split | high-energy interactive labs |
| **Newspaper front page** | Broadsheet with masthead and columns | Playfair Display, Old Standard TT, IBM Plex Serif | newsprint, black ink, one red spot color | off-white paper, column rules, halftone dots | primary-source units, reveals, mysteries |

## Rules that apply to every style

- **One accent color** carries the title. Everything else is the dark ink tone, light paper, and one trim color.
- **Thick dark outlines and hard drop shadows** (no blur) keep text legible at thumbnail size.
- **Real people and characters** go in as sticker cut-outs (white outline plus a hard shadow), cropped so their faces are large.
- **A "hero object"** from the topic (a ship, a train, a map, a document, a machine) gives the cover a sense of place. Draw it as SVG in the same outline style if you have no art.
- **Texture is subtle** (rays, paper grain, halftone at 10–20% contrast), never busy behind text.
- Use the self-hosted fonts from `scripts/fetch_fonts.py`; never rely on the Google Fonts CDN when rendering.
